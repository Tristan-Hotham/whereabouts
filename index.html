        How it works

        Pick what to find. Town: each day's town is one of about 240 of the UK's cities and larger towns, and practice draws from about 560 towns of 15,000 people or more. City: the UK's 73 cities. Village: about 1,200 daily villages of 2,000 to 5,000 people and 2,500 for practice, in England and Wales (the built-up area data I used doesn't cover Scotland and Northern Ireland). County: England's 47 ceremonial counties plus the council areas of Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland, 112 in all; the clues add up the figures for each county's councils. Each mode has its own daily answer, streak and stats, and the daily list reshuffles each time it runs out. London's boroughs are left out as answers.

        After each guess you see the straight-line distance, the direction to the answer and how close you are, from 0% at 1,000 km to 100%. Every wrong guess unlocks the next clue. At the end the answer appears on the map, often with what its name means.

        The clues use the same data as Higher or Lower: ONS and Nomis statistics, Met Office climate averages for 1991 to 2020, Electoral Commission results and counts from my own studies. Name meanings are the usual explanations of common place-name elements, and many names have more than one theory.
